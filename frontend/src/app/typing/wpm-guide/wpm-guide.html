How WPM works

What WPM measures

WPM means words per minute. Real words have very different lengths, so typing tests use a standard word instead: {{ charsPerWord }} characters, spaces and punctuation included. This way, a text full of short words and a text full of long words give comparable speeds.

WPM = (correct characters ÷ {{ charsPerWord }}) ÷ minutes

For example, typing 1,250 correct characters in 5 minutes makes 250 standard words, which is 50 WPM.

Mistakes and accuracy

Only correct characters count. A mistake left in the text adds nothing to your speed, and correcting it with Backspace costs the time it takes. Some tests call this the net speed, as opposed to the gross speed, which counts every key.

The accuracy shown at the end is the share of characters that were right when you typed them. A mistake you corrected still lowers it: accuracy measures how cleanly you type, while WPM measures how fast the right text appears.

The speeds shown during a test

Speed
Your current speed, measured over the last {{ windowSeconds }} seconds. It rises when you speed up and falls when you hesitate.
Average
Your speed since the start of the test. At the end, it is your result.
Highest speed
The highest current speed you reached. It is only measured after the first {{ peakDelaySeconds }} seconds, when a few quick words are not enough to make a record.

Good speeds by age

Typing speed depends far more on practice than on age: a teenager who chats every day can be faster than an adult who rarely types. The ranges below are rough averages reported by typing schools and online typing tests, not scientific norms.
